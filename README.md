# Lavsen Dahal

## About Me

I am a PhD student in Electrical & Computer Engineering at Duke University, working at the [Center for Virtual Imaging Trials](https://cvit.duke.edu/). My research focuses on developing advanced computer vision and AI methods for medical imaging applications.

## A Global Academic Journey

Originally from **Nepal**, my academic journey has been defined by a unique international perspective, having studied and conducted research across **seven countries** on three continents. This diverse training has shaped my approach to research, combining rigorous technical training with cross-cultural collaboration and adaptability.

### Educational Path

My academic journey began in **Nepal**, where I completed my high school education. I then pursued my Bachelor's in Electrical & Electronics Engineering from [VNIT Nagpur](http://vnit.ac.in/) in **India**, building a strong foundation in engineering and technical problem-solving.

Following my undergraduate studies, I was selected as an **Erasmus+ Global Scholar** to pursue a Master's degree in **Medical Imaging and Applications**—a prestigious joint program spanning three European countries:

- **France** ([University of Burgundy](https://www.u-bourgogne.fr/)) - Applied Mathematics and Computer Vision
- **Italy** ([University of Cassino](https://www.unicas.it/)) - Pattern Recognition and Robotics
- **Spain** ([University of Girona](https://www.udg.edu/)) - Medical Imaging

This unique European consortium provided me with comprehensive training across the entire spectrum from foundational mathematics to clinical applications, preparing me for cutting-edge research at the intersection of AI and healthcare.

Currently, I am pursuing my **PhD in Electrical & Computer Engineering** at Duke University in the **United States**, where I continue to push the boundaries of medical imaging AI.

**Educational Progression**: Nepal (High School) → India (Bachelor's) → Europe (Erasmus+ Master's) → USA (PhD)

### Research Experience

My research experience spans prestigious institutions across the globe:

- **VICOROB Lab** (Girona, Spain) - Medical imaging and computer vision
- **BioMedia Lab** at [Imperial College London](https://biomedia.doc.ic.ac.uk/) (United Kingdom) - Advanced medical image analysis
- **NAAMII** ([Nepal Applied Mathematics and Informatics Institute](https://www.naamii.org.np/), Nepal) - Machine learning applications in healthcare
- **CVIT** at [Duke University](https://cvit.duke.edu/) (USA) - Virtual imaging trials and digital twins

### Research Focus

My work spans:
- **Vision-language models** for disease localization
- **Multi-modal segmentation** algorithms
- **Anatomical digital twins** for synthetic imaging experiments
- **Self-supervised and weakly-supervised learning** approaches that reduce annotation burden in medical AI

### What Diverse Training Has Taught Me

Training across seven countries and multiple leading research labs has profoundly shaped my perspective on research and collaboration:

**Cross-Cultural Collaboration**: Working with teams from Nepal to the United States has taught me how to bridge cultural and technical differences, fostering truly collaborative research environments.

**Adaptability**: Navigating different educational systems, research cultures, and methodologies has made me highly adaptable and quick to learn new frameworks and approaches.

**Global Perspective**: Exposure to healthcare challenges across different continents has given me a broader understanding of how medical AI solutions must be robust, generalizable, and culturally sensitive.

**Interdisciplinary Thinking**: Training across diverse institutions—from engineering schools in India to applied mathematics in France, pattern recognition in Italy, medical imaging in Spain, and specialized AI labs in the US—has strengthened my ability to integrate knowledge from multiple disciplines.

**Resilience and Independence**: Living and studying across continents has built resilience, self-reliance, and the confidence to tackle complex research challenges independently while knowing when to seek collaboration.

## Current Work

At Duke University, I am focused on pushing the boundaries of medical imaging AI through:
- Developing open-vocabulary segmentation methods for multi-modal medical imaging
- Creating large-scale libraries of anatomical digital twins for virtual imaging experiments
- Advancing self-supervised learning techniques to reduce the annotation burden in medical AI
